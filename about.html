About RRC-HTA

This page describes RRC-HTA’s role in the HTAIn. For national mandate, governance and objectives, see htain.dhr.gov.in.

The Regional Resource Centre for Health Technology Assessment (RRC-HTAIn) was established at AMCHSS, SCTIMST, Trivandrum on 27 February 2018. It is funded by the Department of Health Research. The mandate is to conduct research, build capacity, and generate quality evidence to support health-policy decisions in Kerala towards Universal Health Coverage.

Mandate

HTAIn operates under the Department of Health Research, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, and generates evidence on the clinical effectiveness, cost-effectiveness and safety of medicines, devices and health programmes.

SCTIMST coordinates with the State Government of Kerala. Prof. Biju Soman is the Principal Investigator. In 2025–26 the centre received the gold medal for best-performing resource centre at ARISE-2025 (10 December 2025, New Delhi).

Relationship to AMCHSS, SCTIMST and HTAIn

  • Achutha Menon Centre for Health Science Studies (AMCHSS) is recognised as a centre of excellence in public health training by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Government of India. The centre conducts training and research across diverse areas of public health.
  • Sree Chitra Tirunal Institute for Medical Sciences and Technology (SCTIMST), Thiruvananthapuram, is an autonomous Institute of National Importance (INI) with university status, established in 1980 under the Department of Science and Technology, Government of India, by an Act of Parliament (Act 52 of 1980).
  • HTAIn Established by the Department of Health Research (DHR), MoHFW, HTAIn evaluates medical interventions, drugs, and health programs to inform evidence-based policy, reimbursement, and resource allocation across India.

What the unit does?

  • Cost-utility analysis (CUA) — Quantifying value for money by measuring the incremental cost per quality-adjusted life year (QALY) gained to determine long-term cost-effectiveness against health-system thresholds.
  • Budget impact analysis (BIA) — Assessing real-world affordability and forecasting the 1- to 5-year financial impact of adopting new technologies within existing healthcare budgets.
  • Decision-analytic modelling — Synthesising clinical and epidemiological data to simulate long-term disease trajectories and cost outcomes:
    • Markov and semi-Markov models: chronic disease staging and long-term progression.
    • Decision trees: short-term interventions and acute diagnostic pathways.
    • Dynamic transmission models: infectious disease dynamics and vaccination strategies.

    Toolstack: R (heemod, hesim), TreeAge Pro, and client-ready Microsoft Excel models.

  • Capacity-building — Fostering health-economics literacy through tailored sensitisation sessions for healthcare leaders and hands-on modelling workshops for researchers and analysts. See Events.

Priorities ahead include completing ongoing HTAs to HTAIn quality standards, expanding free and open-source software for literature review and modelling, and supporting a State HTA cell in Kerala. See Resources.
